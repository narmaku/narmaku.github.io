# Rediscovering Binaural Beats: Exploring Audio for Focus and Wellness

I have recently revisited a topic that fascinated me several years ago: binaural waves (also known as brain waves or binaural beats). 

For those of you not familiar with how this works, these are waves reproduced at specific frequencies in a stereo modality. Essentially, each ear receives a slightly different frequency, which induces the brain to perceive a third "internal" frequency. This process activates specific brain areas, simulating the effects of certain stimulants like caffeine or promoting a deeper sense of relaxation. The best part? It is 100% free, safe, and legal. (Though I should warn you: if you overdo it with high volumes or long sessions, you might end up with a bit of a headache!)

### A Trip Down Memory Lane: The I-Doser Days

I remember being a teenager (around 16 or 17 years old) and reading about this in an old internet forum. Back then, there was a software called **I-Doser** that marketed itself as a pioneer in the field of brain wave audio. That was their whole marketing angle.

I was quite skeptical at the time. I decided to try their free samples, but even after 30-minute sessions, the effects were incredibly subtle. It felt like a minor "relaxing" or "disturbing" sensation. Nothing substantial. Eventually, I just left it aside as a curious anecdote and forgot about it for years.

### The Return: AI Insights and Adult Brains

Fast forward to today. I decided to look into this again, mostly out of random curiosity. Of course, I turned to Generative AI to dig deeper. It is amazing how easily an LLM can break down the therapeutic applications and the science behind these waves. 

One interesting thing the AI pointed out is that as our brains evolve and accumulate years of stress, traumatic experiences, and develop anxiety patterns, we might actually become *more* responsive to these waves. Apparently, as an adult, I don't need to wait 40 minutes to feel the effects like I did when I was a kid.

### My Current Setup and Results

I decided to give it a real shot using high-quality gear. I’ve been using my **SONY WH-1000XM4** noise-canceling headphones combined with an **Apple Music** subscription for that sweet lossless audio. Quality is mandatory here; if you use low-bitrate streams, the effect loses its "punch."

I am highly surprised by the intensity. Even during the first 2-5 minutes I can start feeling the effects. And after listening to a full album, the effects can last for hours. Right now, I’m following an artist called **Magic Tones**. Their *Focus* album has been a great in helping with my productivity. It helps me maintain sharp concentration, see details more clearly (e.g. in images or while reading code), and jump between different technical contexts without that typical "cognitive drain" or fatigue. You know, as I am currently working with multiple AI agent sessions in parallel, this Focus brain waves have been really helpful. And I am not exagerating.

When I need to wind down, I switch to the Anxiety Relief album. It reduces my stress to the point where I feel a physical tingling sensation in my hands and the back of my head. It’s quite a trip! No joke.

### Quick Reference: Frequency Guide

If you want to explore this yourself, here is a breakdown of the most common frequencies you'll find on platforms like TIDAL, Spotify, or Apple Music:

| Frequency Range | Popular Name | Primary Use Cases |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| < 4 Hz | **Delta** | Deep sleep, physical healing, pain relief. |
| 4 – 8 Hz | **Theta** | Deep relaxation, meditation, REM sleep. |
| 8 – 13 Hz | **Alpha** | Relaxed focus, stress reduction, "Flow State." |
| 13 – 32 Hz | **Beta** | High alertness, problem-solving, active thinking. |
| > 32 Hz | **Gamma** | Peak awareness, cognitive enhancement (Use with caution!). |

### My Advice for Beginners

If you are curious about starting your own journey with brain waves, I recommend starting with low-frequency relaxing waves (below 10 Hz). 

IMPORTANT: avoid **Gamma waves (40 Hz)** at the start. They are very powerful and put your brain in a high-alert state that can feel quite uncomfortable or even induce unnecessary anxiety if you aren't used to it. Start slow, use good headphones, and see how your brain reacts!
